Differentiation Rules

Rather than computing every derivative from the limit definition, a set of algebraic rules — power, product, quotient, and chain — let you differentiate any combination of functions quickly and systematically.

Why We Need Rules

Applying the limit definition every time would be tedious for complex functions. The differentiation rules are proved once from the definition and then applied forever — turning differentiation into an algebraic procedure rather than a limit calculation.

The rules compose: once you know the power, product, quotient, and chain rules, you can differentiate any elementary function — polynomials, trigonometric, exponential, and logarithmic — and any combination thereof.

The Core Rules

Power Rule
\[ \frac{d}{dx}[x^n] = nx^{n-1} \]
Works for all real \(n\). E.g. \(\frac{d}{dx}[x^5] = 5x^4\).
Constant & Sum
\[ \frac{d}{dx}[c] = 0, \quad \frac{d}{dx}[f \pm g] = f' \pm g' \]
Constants vanish; derivatives distribute across sums and differences.
Product Rule
\[ \frac{d}{dx}[fg] = f'g + fg' \]
"First times derivative of second, plus second times derivative of first."
Quotient Rule
\[ \frac{d}{dx}\!\left[\frac{f}{g}\right] = \frac{f'g - fg'}{g^2} \]
"Low d-high minus high d-low over low squared."
Chain Rule
\[ \frac{d}{dx}[f(g(x))] = f'(g(x)) \cdot g'(x) \]
Derivative of outer (evaluated at inner) times derivative of inner.
Trig Derivatives
\[ \frac{d}{dx}[\sin x] = \cos x, \quad \frac{d}{dx}[\cos x] = -\sin x \]
Also: \(\frac{d}{dx}[\tan x] = \sec^2 x\).

Worked Examples

Example 1 — Product Rule
Differentiate \(f(x) = x^3 \sin x\)
1
Identify: \(u = x^3\), \(v = \sin x\). So \(u' = 3x^2\), \(v' = \cos x\).
2
Apply product rule: \(f'(x) = u'v + uv' = 3x^2 \sin x + x^3 \cos x\)
✓
\(f'(x) = x^2(3\sin x + x\cos x)\)
Example 2 — Quotient Rule
Differentiate \(\displaystyle f(x) = \frac{x^2 + 1}{x - 3}\)
1
\(f' = \dfrac{(2x)(x-3) - (x^2+1)(1)}{(x-3)^2}\)
2
Expand numerator: \(2x^2 - 6x - x^2 - 1 = x^2 - 6x - 1\)
✓
\(f'(x) = \dfrac{x^2 - 6x - 1}{(x-3)^2}\)
Example 3 — Chain Rule
Differentiate \(f(x) = \sin(3x^2)\)
1
Outer: \(\sin(\cdot)\), inner: \(3x^2\). So \(f'_{\text{outer}} = \cos(\cdot)\), \(g'(x) = 6x\).
2
Chain rule: \(f'(x) = \cos(3x^2) \cdot 6x\)
✓
\(f'(x) = 6x\cos(3x^2)\)

Key Takeaways

◈

The power rule is the workhorse — it applies to every term in a polynomial instantly.

◈

The product and quotient rules let you differentiate combinations without expanding first.

◈

The chain rule handles composition — always identify the outer and inner functions first.

◈

Memorise sin, cos, and tan derivatives — they appear everywhere in physics and engineering.
