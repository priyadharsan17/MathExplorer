Series & Sequences

A sequence is an ordered list of numbers. A series is the sum of a sequence's terms. The central question: as you add more terms, does the running total approach a finite limit, or grow without bound?

Sequences, Partial Sums, and Convergence

Given a sequence \(\{a_n\}\), the \(n\)-th partial sum is \(S_n = \sum_{k=1}^n a_k\). The series \(\sum a_n\) converges if \(\lim_{n\to\infty} S_n = L\) for some finite \(L\); otherwise it diverges.

The geometric series is the archetype of convergence: \(\sum_{n=0}^\infty r^n = \tfrac{1}{1-r}\) when \(|r| < 1\). When \(|r| \ge 1\) the terms do not shrink to zero and the series diverges.

The harmonic series \(\sum 1/n\) is a famous example of divergence despite its terms going to zero — the terms shrink, but not fast enough. A p-series \(\sum 1/n^p\) converges exactly when \(p > 1\).

Taylor series represent smooth functions as power series, enabling polynomial approximations of \(\sin x\), \(\cos x\), and \(e^x\) that are accurate near the expansion point.

Key Series

Geometric Series
\[ \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} r^n = \frac{1}{1-r}, \quad |r| < 1 \]
Converges for \(|r| < 1\); diverges otherwise.
Harmonic Series
\[ \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n} = \infty \quad \text{(diverges)} \]
Classic example: terms → 0 yet the sum diverges.
p-Series
\[ \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n^p} \begin{cases} \text{converges} & p > 1 \\ \text{diverges} & p \le 1 \end{cases} \]
Generalises the harmonic series.
Taylor Series for sin
\[ \sin x = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n x^{2n+1}}{(2n+1)!} = x - \frac{x^3}{6} + \frac{x^5}{120} - \cdots \]
Converges for all \(x\).

Worked Examples

Example 1 — Geometric series sum
Find \(\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^n\)
1
\(r = 1/2\), so \(|r| = 1/2 < 1\): converges.
2
Sum \(= \dfrac{1}{1 - 1/2} = \dfrac{1}{1/2} = 2\).
✓
\(\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} (1/2)^n = 2\)
Example 2 — Taylor approximation of sin(0.5)
Use the first 3 terms of the Taylor series to approximate \(\sin(0.5)\)
1
\(\sin x \approx x - x^3/6 + x^5/120\)
2
\(= 0.5 - 0.125/6 + 0.03125/120 = 0.5 - 0.020833 + 0.000260\)
✓
\(\approx 0.479427\). Exact: \(\sin(0.5) \approx 0.479426\) ✓

Key Takeaways

◈

A series converges when its partial sums approach a finite limit; it diverges when they grow without bound.

◈

For a series to converge, the terms \(a_n\) must approach zero — but that alone is not sufficient (harmonic series).

◈

Geometric series \(\sum r^n\) converges to \(1/(1-r)\) when \(|r| < 1\).

◈

Taylor series let us replace complex functions with polynomials of arbitrary degree near a point.
