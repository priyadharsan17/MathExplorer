Definite Integrals

A definite integral computes the signed area between a function and the x-axis over an interval \([a,b]\). It is defined as the limit of Riemann sums — the sum of rectangle areas as their width approaches zero.

Accumulating Area

Divide the interval \([a,b]\) into \(n\) equal sub-intervals of width \(\Delta x = (b-a)/n\). In each sub-interval pick a representative point \(x_i^*\) and form a rectangle of height \(f(x_i^*)\). The sum of all rectangle areas is the Riemann sum.

As \(n \to \infty\) (more, thinner rectangles) the sum converges to the exact area — the definite integral: \[\int_a^b f(x)\,dx = \lim_{n \to \infty} \sum_{i=1}^{n} f(x_i^*)\,\Delta x\]

Area above the x-axis contributes positively; area below contributes negatively. The definite integral gives the net signed area.

Key Formulas & Properties

Definition
\[ \int_a^b f(x)\,dx = \lim_{n \to \infty} \sum_{i=1}^{n} f(x_i^*)\,\Delta x \]
With \(\Delta x = (b-a)/n\) and \(x_i^* \in [x_{i-1}, x_i]\).
Power Rule for ∫
\[ \int_a^b x^n\,dx = \left[\frac{x^{n+1}}{n+1}\right]_a^b \quad (n \ne -1) \]
Evaluate the antiderivative at \(b\), subtract its value at \(a\).
Linearity
\[ \int_a^b [cf(x) \pm g(x)]\,dx = c\int_a^b f + \int_a^b g \]
Constants factor out; sums split across the integral.
Reversal
\[ \int_a^b f(x)\,dx = -\int_b^a f(x)\,dx \]
Swapping limits negates the integral.

Worked Examples

Example 1 — Basic power integration
Compute \(\displaystyle\int_0^3 x^2\,dx\)
1
Antiderivative of \(x^2\) is \(\dfrac{x^3}{3}\).
2
Evaluate: \(\left[\dfrac{x^3}{3}\right]_0^3 = \dfrac{27}{3} - 0 = 9\)
✓
The area under \(x^2\) from 0 to 3 is \(9\) square units.
Example 2 — Signed area
Compute \(\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1} x^3\,dx\)
1
Antiderivative: \(\dfrac{x^4}{4}\)
2
\(\left[\dfrac{x^4}{4}\right]_{-1}^{1} = \dfrac{1}{4} - \dfrac{1}{4} = 0\)
✓
\(x^3\) is an odd function — area above cancels area below, giving net 0.
Example 3 — Left Riemann sum approximation
Approximate \(\displaystyle\int_0^2 x^2\,dx\) with \(n = 4\) left rectangles.
1
\(\Delta x = 2/4 = 0.5\). Left endpoints: \(0, 0.5, 1.0, 1.5\).
2
Heights: \(0^2 + 0.5^2 + 1^2 + 1.5^2 = 0 + 0.25 + 1 + 2.25 = 3.5\)
3
Sum × \(\Delta x\) = \(3.5 \times 0.5 = 1.75\).
✓
Exact value: \(\frac{8}{3} \approx 2.667\). More rectangles → closer approximation.

Key Takeaways

◈

The definite integral is the limit of Riemann sums — the total signed area between \(f\) and the x-axis.

◈

More rectangles (\(n \to \infty\)) always improves the approximation toward the exact answer.

◈

Midpoint rule generally outperforms left/right rules for the same \(n\).

◈

The Fundamental Theorem of Calculus connects this area to antiderivatives — making exact computation easy.
