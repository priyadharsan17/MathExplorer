Derivatives

The derivative measures the instantaneous rate of change of a function — the slope of its tangent line at any point. It is defined as the limit of a difference quotient as the interval shrinks to zero.

The Idea Behind a Derivative

Imagine drawing a straight line (a secant) through two points on a curve. The slope of this secant is the average rate of change between those points. Now slide the second point toward the first. As the gap \(h \to 0\), the secant rotates and approaches a unique limiting position — the tangent line. Its slope is the derivative at that point.

Formally, the derivative of \(f\) at \(x\) is defined as: \[\displaystyle f'(x) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h}\] This is the difference quotient definition — all differentiation rules flow from it.

Key Definitions & Notation

Limit Definition
\[ f'(x) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h} \]
The foundation — slope of the tangent line at \(x\).
Leibniz Notation
\[ \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{d}{dx}\bigl[f(x)\bigr] \]
Emphasises the derivative as a ratio of infinitesimal changes.
Power Rule
\[ \frac{d}{dx}\bigl[x^n\bigr] = nx^{n-1} \]
Valid for all real \(n\). E.g. \(\frac{d}{dx}[x^2] = 2x\).
Secant Slope
\[ m_{\text{sec}} = \frac{f(x_0 + h) - f(x_0)}{h} \]
Average rate of change over \([x_0, x_0 + h]\). Approaches \(f'(x_0)\) as \(h \to 0\).

Worked Examples

Example 1 — Derivative from the definition
Find \(f'(x)\) for \(f(x) = x^2\) using the limit definition.
1
Write the difference quotient: \(\displaystyle\frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h} = \frac{(x+h)^2 - x^2}{h}\)
2
Expand: \(\displaystyle\frac{x^2 + 2xh + h^2 - x^2}{h} = \frac{2xh + h^2}{h} = 2x + h\)
3
Take the limit: \(\displaystyle\lim_{h \to 0}(2x + h) = 2x\)
✓
\(f'(x) = 2x\). At \(x = 3\), the tangent slope is \(6\).
Example 2 — Using the Power Rule
Differentiate \(f(x) = 4x^3 - 7x + 2\)
1
Apply the power rule term by term: \(\frac{d}{dx}[4x^3] = 12x^2\)
2
\(\frac{d}{dx}[-7x] = -7\), and the constant \(2\) differentiates to \(0\).
✓
\(f'(x) = 12x^2 - 7\)
Example 3 — Tangent line equation
Find the equation of the tangent to \(f(x) = x^2\) at \(x = 2\)
1
Slope: \(f'(2) = 2(2) = 4\)
2
Point: \(f(2) = 4\), so the tangent passes through \((2, 4)\).
3
Point-slope form: \(y - 4 = 4(x - 2)\)
✓
Tangent line: \(y = 4x - 4\)

Key Takeaways

◈

The derivative is the limit of the difference quotient — the instantaneous rate of change at a point.

◈

Geometrically, \(f'(x_0)\) is the slope of the tangent line to the curve at \(x_0\).

◈

If \(f'(x) > 0\) on an interval, the function is increasing; if \(f'(x) < 0\), decreasing.

◈

Not every function is differentiable everywhere — corners, cusps, and discontinuities prevent it.
