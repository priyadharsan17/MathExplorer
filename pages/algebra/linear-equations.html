Linear Equations

A linear equation is any equation that forms a straight line when graphed. Understanding them is the gateway to all of algebra — and to calculus, statistics, and beyond.

What is a Linear Equation?

A linear equation in one variable has the form \( ax + b = 0 \), where \(a \ne 0\). The solution is the single value of \(x\) that makes the equation true.

In two variables, a linear equation takes the form \( y = mx + b \) — the slope-intercept form. Here \(m\) is the slope (steepness and direction) and \(b\) is the y-intercept (where the line crosses the y-axis).

The defining feature of a linear equation is that neither variable is raised to a power other than 1, and no two variables are multiplied together. This is what guarantees a straight line.

Key Formulas

Standard Form
\[ ax + b = 0 \]
Solve by isolating \(x\): \(\displaystyle x = -\frac{b}{a}\)
Slope-Intercept
\[ y = mx + b \]
\(m\) = slope, \(b\) = y-intercept
Slope Formula
\[ m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1} \]
Rise over run between any two points on the line
Point-Slope
\[ y - y_1 = m(x - x_1) \]
Use when you know a point \((x_1, y_1)\) and the slope \(m\)

Worked Examples

Example 1 — Solving a one-variable equation
Solve: \(3x - 9 = 0\)
1
Add 9 to both sides: 3x = 9
2
Divide both sides by 3: x = 3
✓
Check: \(3(3) - 9 = 0\) ✓
Example 2 — Finding slope between two points
Find the slope through \((1, 2)\) and \((4, 8)\)
1
Apply the slope formula: \(\displaystyle m = \frac{8 - 2}{4 - 1}\)
2
Simplify: \(\displaystyle m = \frac{6}{3} = 2\)
✓
The line rises 2 units for every 1 unit moved right.
Example 3 — Writing the equation from a point and slope
Write the equation of a line through \((2, 5)\) with slope \(m = 3\)
1
Use point-slope form: y − 5 = 3(x − 2)
2
Expand: y − 5 = 3x − 6
3
Rearrange to slope-intercept: y = 3x − 1

Key Takeaways

◈

A linear equation always produces a straight line — the variable's highest power is 1.

◈

Slope \(m\) describes direction and steepness. Positive \(m\) → line rises; negative \(m\) → line falls.

◈

The y-intercept \(b\) is the value of \(y\) when \(x = 0\) — where the line crosses the y-axis.

◈

Two lines are parallel when they have the same slope, and perpendicular when their slopes are negative reciprocals.
