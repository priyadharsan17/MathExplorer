Matrices

A matrix organises numbers into rows and columns, enabling compact notation for systems of equations and linear transformations. Explore multiplication, determinants, inverses, and how 2×2 matrices transform the plane.

What is a Matrix?

A matrix is a rectangular array of numbers. A 2×2 matrix has 2 rows and 2 columns: \[ A = \begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{bmatrix} \] Matrices represent linear transformations — they can rotate, scale, shear, and reflect 2D shapes.

The determinant \(\det(A) = ad - bc\) encodes how much the transformation scales areas. If \(\det(A) = 0\), the matrix is singular (non-invertible) and collapses the plane.

Key Formulas — 2×2 Matrix

Matrix Multiplication
\[ \begin{bmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix}e&f\\g&h\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}ae+bg & af+bh \\ ce+dg & cf+dh\end{bmatrix} \]
Determinant
\[ \det(A) = ad - bc \]
Area scale factor of the transformation.
Inverse (if det ≠ 0)
\[ A^{-1} = \frac{1}{ad - bc} \begin{bmatrix} d & -b \\ -c & a \end{bmatrix} \]
Transpose
\[ A^T = \begin{bmatrix}a&c\\b&d\end{bmatrix} \]
Rows become columns.

Worked Examples

Example 1 — Matrix Multiplication
Compute \( \begin{bmatrix}1&2\\3&4\end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix}5&6\\7&8\end{bmatrix} \)
1
Top-left: 1×5 + 2×7 = 5 + 14 = 19
2
Top-right: 1×6 + 2×8 = 6 + 16 = 22
3
Bottom-left: 3×5 + 4×7 = 15 + 28 = 43
4
Bottom-right: 3×6 + 4×8 = 18 + 32 = 50
✓
Result: \( \begin{bmatrix}19&22\\43&50\end{bmatrix} \)
Example 2 — Inverse Matrix
Find \( A^{-1} \) for \( A = \begin{bmatrix}3&1\\5&2\end{bmatrix} \)
1
Determinant: det = 3×2 − 1×5 = 6 − 5 = 1
2
Swap a, d and negate b, c: [2, -1; -5, 3]
3
Multiply by 1/det = 1/1 = 1
✓
\( A^{-1} = \begin{bmatrix}2&-1\\-5&3\end{bmatrix} \)

Key Takeaways

◈

Matrix multiplication is not commutative: \(AB \neq BA\) in general.

◈

The determinant measures the area scale factor. \(\det = 0\) means the transformation collapses the plane.

◈

A negative determinant means the transformation includes a reflection (orientation is reversed).

◈

Every 2×2 linear transformation — rotation, scaling, shearing — is encoded in just 4 numbers.
