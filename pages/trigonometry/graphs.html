Graphs of Trig Functions

Explore how amplitude, period, phase shift, and vertical shift transform the graph of \(y = A\sin(Bx + C) + D\). Every parameter has a precise geometric meaning you can see in action.

The Four Parameters

The general form \(y = A\sin(Bx + C) + D\) (or with cosine) has four parameters:

  • A — Amplitude: vertical stretch/compression. Peak-to-midline distance is \(|A|\).
  • B — Frequency factor: the period becomes \(2\pi/|B|\).
  • C — Phase shift: horizontal shift of \(-C/B\) units.
  • D — Vertical shift: moves the midline from \(y=0\) to \(y=D\).

Changing the sign of \(A\) reflects the graph vertically; changing the sign of \(B\) reflects horizontally.

Key Formulas

General Form
\[ y = A\sin(Bx+C)+D \]
Period
\[ T = \frac{2\pi}{|B|} \]
Phase Shift
\[ \phi = -\frac{C}{B} \quad \text{(horizontal shift)} \]
Range
\[ y \in [D-|A|,\; D+|A|] \]

Worked Examples

Example 1 — Reading parameters
Identify \(A,B,C,D\) for \(y = 3\sin(2x - \pi) + 1\)
1
\(A=3\): amplitude is 3.
2
\(B=2\): period = \(2\pi/2 = \pi\).
3
\(C=-\pi\): phase shift = \(-(-\pi)/2 = \pi/2\) right.
✓
\(D=1\): midline at \(y=1\). Range: \([-2, 4]\).

Key Takeaways

◈

Amplitude \(|A|\) sets the height; period \(2\pi/|B|\) sets the width of one full cycle.

◈

Phase shift \(-C/B\) slides the graph left or right without changing its shape.

◈

Vertical shift \(D\) moves the midline up or down.
