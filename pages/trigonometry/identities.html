Trig Identities

A trigonometric identity is an equation that holds for all values of the variable. Mastering the key families — Pythagorean, reciprocal, sum/difference, and double-angle — unlocks simplification, integration, and equation solving.

Why Identities Matter

Identities let you rewrite an expression in a different form — one that may be easier to evaluate, integrate, or factor. For example, integrating \(\sin^2 x\) directly is hard; replacing it with \((1 - \cos 2x)/2\) makes it straightforward.

The Pythagorean identity \(\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = 1\) and its variants underpin most proofs. The angle-addition formulas are the workhorse of derivation.

Key Formulas

Pythagorean
\[\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1,\quad 1+\tan^2\theta=\sec^2\theta,\quad 1+\cot^2\theta=\csc^2\theta\]
Angle Addition
\[\sin(A\pm B)=\sin A\cos B\pm\cos A\sin B\] \[\cos(A\pm B)=\cos A\cos B\mp\sin A\sin B\]
Double Angle
\[\sin 2A = 2\sin A\cos A,\quad \cos 2A = \cos^2 A - \sin^2 A\]
Half Angle
\[\sin^2 A = \frac{1-\cos 2A}{2},\qquad \cos^2 A = \frac{1+\cos 2A}{2}\]

Worked Examples

Example 1 — Proving a simple identity
Show that \(\tan\theta\cdot\cos\theta = \sin\theta\)
1
Replace \(\tan\theta = \sin\theta/\cos\theta\)
2
\(\dfrac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}\cdot\cos\theta = \sin\theta \cdot\dfrac{\cos\theta}{\cos\theta} = \sin\theta\) ✓
Example 2 — Using double angle to integrate
Simplify \(\displaystyle\int \sin^2 x\,dx\)
1
Use half-angle: \(\sin^2 x = (1-\cos 2x)/2\)
2
\(\displaystyle\int \tfrac{1-\cos 2x}{2}\,dx = \tfrac{x}{2} - \tfrac{\sin 2x}{4} + C\)

Key Takeaways

◈

Always work on one side of an identity — never move terms across the equals sign.

◈

The Pythagorean identity \(\sin^2+\cos^2=1\) and reciprocal identities are the most-used tools.

◈

Double-angle formulas come directly from the angle-addition formulas with \(A=B\).
