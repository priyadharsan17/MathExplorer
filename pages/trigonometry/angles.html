Angles & Radians

Angles can be measured in degrees or radians. Radians are the natural unit in mathematics because arc length = radius × angle in radians — no conversion factor needed.

What Is a Radian?

One radian is the angle subtended at the centre of a circle by an arc equal in length to the radius. Since the full circumference is \(2\pi r\), a full turn is \(2\pi\) radians = 360°.

To convert: multiply degrees by \(\pi/180\) to get radians; multiply radians by \(180/\pi\) to get degrees.

Common angles to memorise: \(30° = \pi/6\), \(45° = \pi/4\), \(60° = \pi/3\), \(90° = \pi/2\), \(180° = \pi\).

Key Formulas

Conversion
\[ \text{rad} = \text{deg} \times \frac{\pi}{180}, \qquad \text{deg} = \text{rad} \times \frac{180}{\pi} \]
Arc Length
\[ s = r\theta \quad (\theta \text{ in radians}) \]
Arc length equals radius times the central angle in radians.
Sector Area
\[ A = \tfrac{1}{2}r^2\theta \]
Area of a circular sector with central angle \(\theta\) radians.
Full Circle
\[ 360° = 2\pi \text{ rad}, \quad 180° = \pi \text{ rad} \]

Worked Examples

Example 1 — Degrees to radians
Convert 150° to radians
1
Multiply by \(\pi/180\): \(150 \times \pi/180\)
✓
\(= 5\pi/6 \approx 2.618\) rad
Example 2 — Arc length
Find the arc length for a radius of 5 and central angle 60°
1
Convert: \(60° = \pi/3\) rad
2
\(s = r\theta = 5 \times \pi/3\)
✓
\(= 5\pi/3 \approx 5.236\)

Key Takeaways

◈

Radians are the natural unit: arc length \(= r\theta\) with no extra factor.

◈

One full revolution \(= 360° = 2\pi\) rad; a right angle \(= 90° = \pi/2\) rad.

◈

Memorise: 30°=π/6, 45°=π/4, 60°=π/3, 90°=π/2, 180°=π.
