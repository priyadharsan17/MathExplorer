Sine & Cosine

Sine and cosine describe the vertical and horizontal positions of a point spinning around the unit circle. Their graphs are the archetypal periodic waves found everywhere in physics, engineering, and music.

From Circle to Wave

As an angle \(\theta\) increases from 0 to \(2\pi\), the point \((\cos\theta, \sin\theta)\) sweeps once around the unit circle. Plotting \(\sin\theta\) against \(\theta\) produces a smooth wave that starts at 0, rises to 1 at \(\pi/2\), returns to 0 at \(\pi\), falls to −1 at \(3\pi/2\), and repeats. Cosine is the same wave shifted left by \(\pi/2\).

Key properties: period \(= 2\pi\), amplitude \(= 1\), range \([-1, 1]\). Both functions are continuous and differentiable everywhere.

Key Formulas

Relationship
\[ \cos\theta = \sin\!\left(\tfrac{\pi}{2} - \theta\right),\quad \sin\theta = \cos\!\left(\tfrac{\pi}{2} - \theta\right) \]
Even / Odd
\[ \cos(-\theta) = \cos\theta \quad \text{(even)}, \quad \sin(-\theta) = -\sin\theta \quad \text{(odd)} \]
Derivatives
\[ \frac{d}{d\theta}\sin\theta = \cos\theta, \quad \frac{d}{d\theta}\cos\theta = -\sin\theta \]
Pythagorean Identity
\[ \sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = 1 \]

Worked Examples

Example 1 — Evaluating with the unit circle
Find \(\cos(5\pi/3)\)
1
\(5\pi/3 = 300°\) → Q4, reference angle \(60°\)
2
In Q4, cosine is positive. \(\cos 60° = 1/2\)
✓
\(\cos(5\pi/3) = 1/2\)
Example 2 — Using odd/even properties
Simplify \(\sin(-\pi/6)\)
1
sin is odd: \(\sin(-\pi/6) = -\sin(\pi/6)\)
✓
\(= -1/2\)

Key Takeaways

◈

Sine and cosine both have period \(2\pi\) and range \([-1,1]\).

◈

Cosine is sine shifted left by \(\pi/2\) — they are the same wave, out of phase.

◈

The derivative of sine is cosine, and vice versa (with a sign flip for cosine).
