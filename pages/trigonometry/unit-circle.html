Unit Circle

The unit circle — radius 1, centred at the origin — is the foundation of trigonometry. Every point on it is \((\cos\theta, \sin\theta)\), directly linking angle to coordinate.

The Unit Circle Defined

For any angle \(\theta\), draw a ray from the origin. Where it intersects the unit circle gives the point \((\cos\theta, \sin\theta)\). All six trig functions follow:

\[\sin\theta = y, \quad \cos\theta = x, \quad \tan\theta = \frac{y}{x} \quad (x \ne 0)\] \[\csc\theta = \frac{1}{y}, \quad \sec\theta = \frac{1}{x}, \quad \cot\theta = \frac{x}{y}\]

The four quadrants determine signs: ASTC — All positive (Q1), Sine positive (Q2), Tangent positive (Q3), Cosine positive (Q4).

Key Formulas

Unit Circle Equation
\[ x^2 + y^2 = 1 \]
Pythagorean Identity
\[ \sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = 1 \]
The Pythagorean theorem on the unit circle.
Special Values
\[ \sin 30° = \tfrac{1}{2},\; \sin 45° = \tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2},\; \sin 60° = \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \]
Cosines are the same list reversed.
Periodicity
\[ \sin(\theta + 2\pi) = \sin\theta, \quad \cos(\theta + 2\pi) = \cos\theta \]

Worked Examples

Example 1 — Reading the unit circle
Find \(\sin 135°\) and \(\cos 135°\)
1
135° is in Quadrant II (reference angle 45°).
2
In Q2: sin positive, cos negative.
✓
\(\sin 135° = \tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\), \(\cos 135° = -\tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\)
Example 2 — Using the Pythagorean identity
If \(\cos\theta = 3/5\) and \(\theta\) is in Q4, find \(\sin\theta\)
1
\(\sin^2\theta = 1 - \cos^2\theta = 1 - 9/25 = 16/25\)
2
Q4 ⟹ sin is negative.
✓
\(\sin\theta = -4/5\)

Key Takeaways

◈

Every point on the unit circle is \((\cos\theta, \sin\theta)\) for some angle \(\theta\).

◈

ASTC reminds you which functions are positive in each quadrant.

◈

The Pythagorean identity \(\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = 1\) is just the unit-circle equation.
